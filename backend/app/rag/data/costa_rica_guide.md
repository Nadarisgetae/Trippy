# Costa Rica Eco-Tourism & Rainforest Expeditions Guide

## Overview & Best Neighborhoods
Costa Rica is a global leader in biodiversity, eco-tourism, and conservation, packed with volcanic ranges, misty cloud forests, and tropical Pacific coastlines. The La Fortuna region serves as the adventure hub at the base of the majestic Arenal Volcano, renowned for geothermal hot springs, suspended canopy walkways, and cascading jungle waterfalls. Further south along the central Pacific coastline lies Manuel Antonio, characterized by turquoise coves, lush coastal rainforests directly meeting white-sand beaches, and diverse wildlife including three-toed sloths, white-faced capuchin monkeys, and scarlet macaws. For high-altitude biodiversity, the Monteverde Cloud Forest reserve bridges the continental divide with perpetual mist, hanging moss, and elusive resplendent quetzals.

## Signature Cultural & Outdoor Excursions
Arenal Volcano National Park offers scenic lava trail hikes through old lava flows from the 1968 eruption alongside panoramic views of Lake Arenal. The Mistico Hanging Bridges park allows travelers to traverse canopy suspension bridges 45 meters above the primary rainforest floor, spot toucans and coatis, or take twilight night walks to observe tree frogs and nocturnal insects. Relaxation is found in natural geothermal hot spring rivers such as Tabacón or EcoTermales. In Manuel Antonio National Park, guided morning nature walks lead through coastal rainforest directly to Playa Manuel Antonio and Gemelas beach, where gentle waves are ideal for swimming and snorkeling. Adventure travelers can also experience class III-IV whitewater rafting on the Balsa or Pacuare rivers and zipline canopy tours across tropical canyons.

## Local Gastronomy & Culinary Etiquette
Costa Rican cuisine focuses on wholesome, fresh tropical ingredients and hearty culinary traditions centered around the concept of "Pura Vida" (pure life). The quintessential national breakfast is *gallo pinto* (seasoned rice and black beans stir-fried with onions, bell peppers, and *Salsa Lizano*, served with fried sweet plantains, eggs, and fresh local cheese). For lunch and dinner, traditional *sodas* (small family-run eateries) serve *casados*—balanced platters containing rice, black beans, salad, fried plantains, and grilled fish, chicken, or braised beef. Fresh tropical fruits including papaya, passion fruit (maracuyá), mango, and guanábana are served as refreshing natural fruit juices (*frescos* or *batidos*). Tipping is customarily a 10% service charge already included on restaurant bills, though an extra 5–10% for exceptional personal service is warmly welcomed.

## Transit & Navigation Logistics
The primary international gateway is Juan Santamaría International Airport (SJO) in Alajuela near San José, with Daniel Oduber Quirós International Airport (LIR) in Liberia serving northwestern Guanacaste. Driving between destinations requires awareness of topography: the mountain route between San José and Arenal takes roughly 3 to 3.5 hours, while traveling between Arenal and Manuel Antonio requires approximately 4.5 to 5 hours. Renting a 4WD or SUV vehicle is highly recommended due to unpaved gravel stretches around Lake Arenal and Monteverde, alongside river crossings during rainy seasons. Alternatively, reliable shared inter-city tourist shuttle services (such as Interbus or RideCR) and domestic flights via SANSA offer stress-free transfers between major eco-tourism zones.

## Practical Travel Tips & Warnings
Costa Rica experiences two distinct seasons: the dry season (*verano*) from December through April, and the green/rainy season (*invierno*) from May through November, during which mornings are typically bright and sunny followed by heavy afternoon tropical rain showers. Manuel Antonio National Park is strictly closed on Tuesdays for environmental conservation and habitat rest; admission tickets must be booked online in advance via the official SINAC portal because daily visitor capacity is capped. To protect native wildlife, feeding or touching wild animals is strictly illegal and subject to heavy fines. Always carry insect repellent, reef-safe sunscreen, a lightweight waterproof rain shell, and sturdy closed-toe hiking shoes for muddy jungle trails. Tap water is generally safe to drink in major urban and tourist centers, though bottled or filtered water is prudent in remote rural areas.
